Listening guide

Choose Play to listen here. If a stream cannot play in your browser, use Listening page. Live radio uses internet data; FM and AM use a radio receiver.

kbps · Data rate
Kilobits per second. At 128 kbps, listening uses about 58 MB per hour, excluding connection overhead. Higher bitrate is not a quality score across different formats or broadcasts.
MP3, AAC and AAC+ · Audio formats
These formats compress audio for streaming. AAC+ is commonly called HE-AAC. Sound also depends on the original broadcast and encoding; a larger number does not always sound better.
Mono and stereo
Mono has one audio channel; stereo has two. Stereo can create a sense of space, but does not guarantee better sound.
FM, AM, MHz and kHz
FM and AM are ways to broadcast over the air. MHz and kHz are frequency units used to tune a physical radio. Frequencies depend on your location.
M3U, HLS and stream URLs
A stream URL points to live audio. M3U is a playlist of addresses, not a music download. HLS delivers audio in segments, often through an .m3u8 address; browser support varies.
When playback fails
Streams can move, go offline or be restricted. HTTP streams cannot play inside this HTTPS site. Use the listening page or copy the stream URL into a compatible player. Playback checks are snapshots, not guarantees.
